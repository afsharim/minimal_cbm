Okaukuejo Waterhole Zebras Scatter


+---------------+
|  DESCRIPTION  |
+---------------+
In Etosha, each day of the dry season a scene unfolds itself at the open waterholes.  A few brave (or very thirsty) animals come in, unsure of the predator situation, they come in slowly and vigilantly.  Nothing happens, and they go drink, others watch them and move in too.  As the group builds, the confidence goes up and animals move in more freely.

<b>But</b> the reality never escapes them completely - something in the back of their head constantly reminds them that there are lions around.

So they remain on edge - in the knowledge that - as so many cartoons have portrayed - they only need to be faster than their neighbor if a lion was to come in.

So with nerves twitched, they drink and every once in a while someone steps wrong on a stone or splashes just the wrong way, and the <b>whole waterhole erupts into a panic!</b>

Within seconds calm is restored, and everyone drinks again, nerves increasingly twitched - and throughout much of the hot time of the day this scene plays itself out.

This picture was at Okaukuejo, but it happens at waterholes like Okondeka, Gemsbokvlakte, Rietfontein, Goas and Chudop every day for about 9 months of the year.

If you say the word 'Etosha', this is what it conjures up in my mind - the splashing, the sound of the noisy Zebras, the white limestone dust, the noise of those limestone rocks being kicked over.  Jackals look for chances at anything.  Giraffe watch - just to cool for all the mayhem (with some luck nobody will notice the giraffe legs I cut off at the back of the picture).  Doves and other birds trying to get their bit of the action.  That is <b>'Etosha'</b> to me.
